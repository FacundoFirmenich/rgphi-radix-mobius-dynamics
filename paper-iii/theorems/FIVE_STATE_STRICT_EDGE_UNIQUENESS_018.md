# RGΦ Round 018-A — global uniqueness of the strict edge at cardinality five

Let \(F\subset\mathbb Q\cap(1/2,1)\) be a closed strongly connected component of cardinality five for the radix–Möbius semigroup generated by \(T_B,S_B\).  Assume at least one restricted generator is nonpermutational.

## Theorem

\[
\boxed{\text{Exactly one of the ten functional edges on }F\text{ is strict.}}
\]

Thus every five-state nonpermutation all-high SCC is globally defect-one.  The permutation/lollipop theorem of Round 015 applies without an extra hypothesis.

## Step 1 — no simple cycle contains two strict edges

Every strict reduction factor is at least two.  On a simple cycle \(C\) of at most five vertices, the cycle budget gives
\[
R_C<2^{\min(m_T,m_S)}\le 2^2=4.
\]
Two strict edges would give \(R_C\ge4\), impossible.  Hence any pair of strict edges must lie on no common simple directed cycle.

## Step 2 — exhaustive raw functional enumeration

The binding five-state gate has 156,250 labelled assignments:

- 31,250 Hamiltonian `TSTSS` assignments;
- 125,000 `TSTS` four-cycle-plus-one assignments.

After strong connectivity, crossing, diameter and cycle-budget conditions, 4,071 marked maps remain before factor duplication.  Their strict-edge compatibility graphs have independence number at most two.  Therefore a five-state candidate has at most two strict edges.

Allowing a second factor-two or factor-three edge and quotienting by all vertex relabellings and generator exchange produces exactly
\[
\boxed{840}
\]
canonical two-strict profiles.

## Step 3 — exact profile adjudication

The 840 profiles partition exactly as follows.

| adjudication | profiles |
|---|---:|
| high-domain infeasible, exact Farkas certificate | 728 |
| forced rational-state collision | 56 |
| violation of unreduced-fibre injectivity | 50 |
| reaches arithmetic | 6 |

The 728 certificates are rational nonnegative combinations of the margin-one high inequalities whose left-hand side is zero and whose right-hand side is negative.

## Step 4 — the six arithmetic survivors

### Double strict \(T\) into one target

Four labelled factor variants reduce, after quotient, to the maps
\[
T=(0,0,0,4,3),\qquad S=(3,2,4,0,1),
\]
with strict edges \(1\to0\) and \(2\to0\), of factors \(r,s\in\{2,3\}\).

The `S` cycles force \(p_1=p_2=C\); the `T` fibres give \(q_1=rQ,q_2=sQ\).

If \(r=s\), states 1 and 2 coincide.  If \(r\ne s\), the two strict radix congruences imply modulo \(Q\)
\[
BC\equiv rA\equiv sA\pmod Q.
\]
Since \(\gcd(A,Q)=1\) and \(|r-s|=1\), this forces \(Q=1\), impossible.

### One strict \(T\) and one strict \(S\) at the same source

The remaining four factor variants occur in two target patterns with
\[
T=(0,0,1,4,3),
\]
and source state
\[
x_1=\frac{sA}{rQ}.
\]
If \(r=s\), the source reduces to \(A/Q=x_0\), a collision.  If \(r\ne s\), the fixed edge \(T(x_0)=x_0\) gives \(B\equiv1\pmod Q\), while strict \(T(x_1)=x_0\) gives
\[
Bs\equiv r\pmod Q.
\]
Therefore \(Q\mid(r-s)\), again forcing \(Q=1\).

No two-strict profile is realizable.  Since three strict edges are combinatorially impossible, exactly one strict edge remains.

## Authority

The theorem is computer-assisted only in the finite labelled-map/profile enumeration and the 728 exact Farkas certificates.  The final six cases are excluded symbolically.  All source assignments, canonical profiles, certificates and checkers are included in the Round-018 bundle.
