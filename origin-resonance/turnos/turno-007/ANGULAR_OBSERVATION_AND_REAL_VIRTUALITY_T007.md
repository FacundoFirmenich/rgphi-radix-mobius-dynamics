# Angular observation, inaccessible cap and qualitative phase key

Let a unit-circle state be parametrized by `theta in (0,pi)`. The observation

\[
O(\theta)=\sin\theta
\]

is a real coordinate of the state and therefore remains inside reality, but it is not the full state. It is a map or real virtuality: coupled to the underlying geometry yet noninjective.

For a central angle `theta`,

\[
A_{sector}=\theta/2,
\qquad
A_{map}=\sin\theta/2,
\]

so the inaccessible curved cap is

\[
A_{cap}=(\theta-\sin\theta)/2,
\]

and its relative fraction is

\[
\eta(\theta)=1-\frac{\sin\theta}{\theta}=\theta^2/6+O(\theta^4).
\]

At zero angle no area is observed. For positive angle the triangular projection is asymptotically coupled to, but not identical with, the sector.

The observation `sin(theta)` has the two branches `theta` and `pi-theta`. The qualitative key

\[
K(\theta)=\mathbf1_{\{\cos\theta<0\}}
\]

makes

\[
\theta\mapsto(\sin\theta,K(\theta))
\]

injective on `(0,pi)`. The key does not supply the whole hidden geometry; it supplies the branch phase required to synchronize the projection with its complete angular state.
