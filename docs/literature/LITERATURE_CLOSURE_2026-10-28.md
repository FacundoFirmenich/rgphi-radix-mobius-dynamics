# RGΦ literature-positioning closure — 28 October 2026

## Positioning principle

RGΦ must be positioned as a compositional contribution. It does not invent radix transformations, open dynamical systems, Möbius maps, Farey algorithms, p-adic dynamics, dynamic programming or computer-assisted proof. Its candidate novelty is the exact interaction of these structures in one controlled rational semigroup.

## Closest current literatures

### Open radix and beta dynamics

Recent work on beta transformations with fixed or moving holes studies survivor sets, dimensions and parameter dependence. This is the nearest established context for escape under radix maps. RGΦ differs by adding a second Möbius-conjugate action and asking an exact controlled first-hitting question over rational states.

### Farey and projective continued fractions

Recent classification work on projective two-branch continued-fraction algorithms constrains broad novelty claims about “new Farey maps.” RGΦ should use Farey language only for exact determinant geometry and neighbor relations, unless a formal conjugacy or classification result is proved.

### Piecewise Möbius systems

Current work studies structural stability, hyperbolicity and expansivity of piecewise Möbius transformations. RGΦ contributes arithmetic exactness, partial branch admissibility, finite reductions and Bellman control rather than a general stability theory.

### p-adic and profinite dynamics

Recent rational-interpreter and p-adic continued-fraction papers establish broad frameworks for finite-state realization, CRT decomposition and non-Archimedean arithmetic. RGΦ's narrower contribution is a prime-wise reduction ledger coupled to the rational branch matrices and target-control problem.

### Ergodic optimization

Optimization for beta and open multivalued systems is an active area. RGΦ's Bellman problem is finite-horizon and first-passage, whereas ergodic optimization typically concerns invariant measures maximizing long-run observables. The manuscript should compare them explicitly and not use “optimization” generically.

### Computer-assisted proof

Recent certificate audits show that exact computations can remain wrong or unauditable when generators and checkers share assumptions or when archival artifacts are incomplete. RGΦ should make the search engine untrusted, publish a compact checker, preserve canonical hashes and enable hostile clean-room reproduction.

## Priority-risk map

- **Low risk:** the exact benchmark tuple and its explicit nonnesting inequalities, provided the computation is independently verified.
- **Medium risk:** threshold robustness and finite trap mechanisms; related methods exist, but the exact RGΦ results appear specific.
- **Medium-high risk:** Farey/p-adic hidden-state interpretation; broad parent literatures are substantial.
- **High risk if overclaimed:** “new Farey map,” “new p-adic interpreter,” “universal Bellman state,” or physical/ontological implications.
- **Open priority:** no claim of global first occurrence should be made until MathSciNet, zbMATH, citation chaining and specialist queries are complete.
