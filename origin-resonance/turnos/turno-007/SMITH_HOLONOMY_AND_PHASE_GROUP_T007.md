# Smith normal form of content holonomy

Let `G=(V,E)` be a finite strongly connected labelled graph and let

\[
W:Z_1(G;\mathbb Z)\to\mathbb Z^r
\]

be the prime-valuation content map. Put `L_G=W(Z_1(G;Z))`. If the Smith normal form of an integral generator matrix has nonzero diagonal entries

\[
s_1\mid s_2\mid\cdots\mid s_k,
\]

then the finite saturation quotient is

\[
\operatorname{sat}(L_G)/L_G\cong\bigoplus_i\mathbb Z/s_i\mathbb Z.
\]

This is the content phase group. The equal-length phase group is defined analogously from `ker(partial) cap ker(length)`.

## Known five-state component

There is one strict edge `S_3` with valuation `e_3`; every other edge has zero receipt. Five simple cycles have valuation zero and the mixed four-cycle has valuation `e_3`. Hence

\[
L_F=\mathbb Z e_3.
\]

The equal-length closed-walk difference `(TSTS)-(TTTT)` also has receipt `e_3`, so

\[
L_F^{(0)}=\mathbb Z e_3.
\]

Both Smith diagonals are `[1]`. Thus the component has nonzero free holonomy but no finite saturation torsion. Future components may carry both a free pitch lattice and a finite qualitative phase group.
