# OR-17 — projective centrum and boundary involution

Round 022 introduces

\[
C=2A-z,
\qquad
D=2A-w.
\]

In homogeneous coordinates this is the integral linear map

\[
U=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&0&0\\
2&-1&0\\
2&0&-1
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

It satisfies

\[
\boxed{U^2=I},
\qquad
\boxed{\det U=1}.
\]

Thus it is a unimodular involution: it preserves the primitive lattice and is its own inverse.

## Two distinguished projective points

The common elliptic group origin

\[
O=[0:1:-1]
\]

satisfies

\[
UO=-O,
\]

so it is fixed projectively.

The universal boundary point

\[
R=[1:2:2]
\]

is sent to

\[
\boxed{UR=[1:0:0]}.
\]

Hence the transformation simultaneously:

- preserves the group origin in projective space;
- sends the boundary point to the coordinate vertex where both defect coordinates `C,D` vanish.

## Projective-origin interpretation

The primitive lattice states are rays from the excluded lattice origin `(0,0)`. Primitive normalization forgets radial scale; the content cocycle records the scale removed.

This gives an exact mathematical analogue of a centrum that is not itself a state but is necessary to define:

- rays;
- projective identity;
- radial content;
- the distinction between direction and scale.

The analogy is structural and projective. It is not an ontological identification with the user's broader centrum concept.
