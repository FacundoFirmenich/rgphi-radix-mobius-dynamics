# OR-11 — common origin–boundary skeleton of the nineteen cubics

The family is a cubic pencil:

\[
F_n=F_0+nA(2A-z)(2A-w).
\]

Put

\[
C=2A-z,
\qquad D=2A-w.
\]

A point belongs to every member iff

\[
ACD=0,
\qquad F_0=0.
\]

The restrictions factor exactly as

\[
F_0|_{A=0}=-4(w+z)(w^2-wz+z^2),
\]

\[
F_0|_{z=2A}=-4(w-11A)(w-2A)(w+7A),
\]

\[
F_0|_{w=2A}=-4(z-3A)(z-2A)(z-A).
\]

Hence the base locus over the algebraic closure consists of nine points counted with multiplicity:

- `O=[0:1:-1]`;
- the two conjugate points `[0:1:(1±sqrt(-3))/2]`;
- `[1:2:11]`;
- `[1:2:-7]`;
- `[1:3:2]`;
- `[1:1:2]`;
- `R=[1:2:2]` counted twice.

Thus there are six distinct rational base points. In particular,

\[
\boxed{O=[0:1:-1]}
\]

is the group-law origin, while

\[
\boxed{R=[1:2:2]}
\]

is the universal coprimality boundary.

The pair is common to all nineteen curves. `O` has a zero coordinate and a polarized `+1/-1` pair; `R` has the symmetric ratio `1:2:2`.

The pencil/base-locus statement is `CLOSED_SYMBOLIC`. Its interpretation through centrum and dual-helicoid prearchitecture remains branch-level and non-canonical.
