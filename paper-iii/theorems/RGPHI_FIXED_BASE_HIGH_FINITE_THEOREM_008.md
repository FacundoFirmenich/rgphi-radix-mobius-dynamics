# RGΦ Fixed-Base High Depth 008 — finiteness theorem

Let \(B\ge2\) be fixed and let \(G\subset\mathbb Q\cap[0,1]\) be a finite
closed trap for the partial semigroup generated by
\[
T_B(x)=\{Bx\},\qquad S_B=H\circ T_B\circ H^{-1},\qquad H(x)=\frac1{1+x}.
\]
Let \(\mathcal B^\forall_B(G)\) be the maximal universal eventual-entry basin
and let
\[
\mathcal H_B(G)=\mathcal B^\forall_B(G)\cap[1/2,1)
\]
be its high part.

## Theorem 008-A — fixed-base high finiteness

\[
\boxed{\mathcal H_B(G)\text{ is finite}.}
\]
Consequently there is a finite bound
\[
\boxed{K(B,G)<\infty}
\]
on the universal predecessor rank of every high basin state.

### Lemma 1 — infinite high set implies an infinite S-chain
The exact predecessor operator maps finite sets to finite sets. Every high
state has an admissible \(S_B\)-child, that child is high, and universal rank
strictly decreases along it. Hence an infinite high basin would give, by
König's lemma, an infinite sequence of distinct high states
\[
x_0,x_1,x_2,\ldots,\qquad S_B(x_{n+1})=x_n.
\]

### Lemma 2 — fixed finite prime universe
For reduced \(x=p/q\),
\[
\operatorname{num}(S_Bx)=\frac{p}{\gcd(p,B)},\qquad
\operatorname{den}(T_Bx)=\frac{q}{\gcd(q,B)}.
\]
Thus primes outside those dividing \(B\) can neither disappear from a
numerator along an S-chain nor from a denominator along the deterministic
T-segment toward the trap. Every numerator and denominator of a high universal
state has all prime factors in the fixed finite set
\[
\mathscr S_0=\operatorname{PrimeDiv}\!\left(
B\prod_{g\in G}\operatorname{num}(g)\operatorname{den}(g)
\right).
\]

### Lemma 3 — stabilized numerator multiplier
For an infinite S-chain write \(x_n=p_n/q_n\) reduced. Then
\[
p_{n+1}=a_np_n,\qquad a_n=\gcd(p_{n+1},B)\mid B.
\]
The set of primes of \(B\) occurring in \(p_n\) is monotone along the
backward chain. After a finite prefix it stabilizes. For each prime
\(\ell\mid B\) already present, if \(e_\ell=v_\ell(B)\) then
\[
v_\ell(p_{n+1})=v_\ell(p_n)+e_\ell.
\]
Hence on a tail
\[
\boxed{p_{n+1}=A p_n}
\]
for one fixed divisor
\[
A=\prod_{\ell\in P}\ell^{v_\ell(B)}\mid B.
\]
If \(A=1\), the numerator is eventually constant, leaving only finitely many
high reduced fractions. Thus an infinite distinct chain requires \(A>1\).
Put \(C=B/A\).

### Lemma 4 — S-unit reduction
Let \(d_n\in\{0,\ldots,B-1\}\) be the S-branch digit of \(x_{n+1}\). The
unreduced formula is
\[
S_B(x_{n+1})=
\frac{p_{n+1}}{Bq_{n+1}-(B-1+d_n)p_{n+1}}.
\]
On the stabilized tail the reduction factor is exactly \(A\). Therefore
\[
q_n=Cq_{n+1}-(B-1+d_n)p_n.
\]
Set
\[
k_n=B-1+d_n\in\{B-1,\ldots,2B-2\}.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{Cq_{n+1}-q_n=k_np_n.}
\]
Enlarge \(\mathscr S_0\) by the prime divisors of all integers
\(B-1,\ldots,2B-2\); call the result \(\mathscr S\). Then
\[
u_n=\frac{q_n}{Cq_{n+1}},\qquad
v_n=\frac{k_np_n}{Cq_{n+1}}
\]
are nonzero rational \(\mathscr S\)-units satisfying
\[
\boxed{u_n+v_n=1.}
\]
The classical binary S-unit theorem gives only finitely many such pairs for a
fixed finite \(\mathscr S\). Since \(k_n\) has only finitely many values, an
infinite chain would contain two indices with the same \(k\) and the same
normalized S-unit pair. Those equalities imply
\[
\frac{p_n}{q_n}=\frac{p_m}{q_m},
\]
contradicting distinctness. Hence no infinite S-chain exists and
\(\mathcal H_B(G)\) is finite. ∎

## Corollary 008-B — pointwise finite, globally unbounded
Round 007 proved that for the fixed trap shape \(\{1/3,2/3\}\), every
prescribed high depth occurs for infinitely many bases. Round 008 proves that
each individual base has finite high depth. Therefore
\[
\boxed{\forall B,\ K(B,G_B)<\infty,}
\qquad
\boxed{\sup_B K(B,G_B)=\infty.}
\]

## Corollary 008-C — effectivity
Two-variable S-unit equations over number fields admit effective height bounds
and algorithms. Therefore \(K(B,G)\) is effective in principle. A coarse
system-independent envelope can be obtained by combining an explicit bound on
the number of S-unit solutions with the finitely many numerator-support
phases and the finite T-orbits on bounded denominators.

This package does not claim a sharp practical formula. Deriving a much
smaller RGΦ-specific \(K_{\rm eff}(B,G)\) is the next optimization problem.

## Evidence boundary
The only deep external ingredient is the classical finiteness/effectivity
theory for binary S-unit equations. The reduction from the radix–Möbius basin
to that equation is elementary and exact. The low basin may remain infinite
and fractal.
