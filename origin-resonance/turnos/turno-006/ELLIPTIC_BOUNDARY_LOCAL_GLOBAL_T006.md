# T006-F — the boundary return is local, not a global multiple relation

On the `n=17` boundary cubic, take
\[
O=[0:1:-1],\qquad
P=[64233:116725:127328],\qquad
R=[1:0:0].
\]

## Good reduction modulo seven

The reduction is smooth and
\[
\#E(\mathbb F_7)=10.
\]
Exact group arithmetic gives
\[
\operatorname{ord}(\bar P)=5,
\qquad
\operatorname{ord}(\bar R)=10.
\]
Hence
\[
\bar R\notin\langle\bar P\rangle.
\]
If `R=mP` held over `Q`, reduction at seven would put `Rbar` in the subgroup generated by `Pbar`. Therefore
\[
\boxed{R\notin\langle P\rangle\subset E(\mathbb Q).}
\]
The local identity `44 Pbar=Rbar` modulo 83 is not a global integer-multiple relation.

## Shared local relation and global obstruction point

At both `p=7` and `p=83`,
\[
2\bar R=-\bar P.
\]
Globally,
\[
Q=2R+P
\]
is the nonzero point
\[
[84507535659963098565309:
369204931320206918906825:
-66883811065922949873056].
\]
It satisfies
\[
\gcd(A_Q,C_Q+D_Q)
=22873516239
=3\cdot7\cdot83\cdot2411\cdot5443.
\]
Thus the repeated local identity is explained by the nonzero global point `Q` entering reduction kernels at those primes; it is not a global equality.

An exact finite-reduction table through `p<=199` accompanies this theorem.
